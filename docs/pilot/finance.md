# eira Pilot — Finance

## Status

Working financing strategy for the initial Mumford pilot. Financial assumptions and funding mechanisms should be revised as the pilot produces evidence about actual costs, educational value and operational requirements.

## Initial financing model

The initial eira pilot will most likely be financed primarily through private donations.

At this stage, the objective is to fund a small, concrete educational experiment rather than build a large organisation in advance. Funding should therefore be directed as closely as possible towards the actual requirements of the pilot: student devices, protective equipment, a teacher laptop, shared connectivity, AI inference, educational materials and a modest contingency for repairs and unexpected costs.

The current working budget for the three-month, five-student pilot is approximately **25,000 SEK**. See `budget.md` for the detailed assumptions.

Private donations are appropriate for this stage because the pilot is deliberately small and exploratory. The purpose of the first funding round is to make the experiment possible and to learn whether eira provides enough educational value to justify continuation and expansion.

## What donations fund

Initial donations may support:

- five student Android devices
- protective cases and screen protectors
- a teacher laptop
- shared internet connectivity
- frontier-model AI inference and related software costs
- curriculum and RAG materials
- replacement accessories and minor repairs
- other directly observed pilot costs

Where possible, expenditure and actual operating costs should be documented so that donors and future partners can understand what resources were required and what was learned from them.

## Funding principle

**Fund the experiment before funding the institution.**

The first objective is to determine whether eira can provide useful, reliable and locally relevant AI-supported homework assistance in Mumford. Organisational growth should follow evidence from real use rather than precede it.

The pilot should therefore remain small enough that its costs, failures, benefits and operational requirements can be understood clearly.

## Longer-term direction

If the Mumford pilot demonstrates educational value and eira proceeds towards larger-scale deployment in Ghana, the intended long-term direction is to establish an appropriate **Ghanaian non-governmental organisation (NGO)** to support the educational work locally.

A Ghanaian organisation could eventually provide a local institutional home for activities such as fundraising, partnerships, educational programmes, device deployment, local employment and training, and collaboration with schools, communities and other Ghanaian organisations.

The precise legal and organisational form should not be predetermined by the pilot. It should be investigated and established when the scale and evidence justify doing so, with appropriate Ghanaian governance, legal advice and local participation.

## Future financing

If the pilot succeeds, financing may broaden beyond individual private donations. Potential future sources can include philanthropic donors, foundations, educational grants, institutional partnerships, corporate support and other forms of non-profit funding compatible with eira's educational purpose.

The project should avoid designing its operating model around the assumption that expensive AI inference or infrastructure will always be subsidised. Cost per student, inference cost, connectivity, hardware lifetime and local support requirements should be measured from the beginning.

## Financial questions to measure

- capital cost per student
- recurring cost per student
- AI inference cost per active student
- connectivity cost for the shared site
- expected device lifetime
- repair and replacement cost
- teacher and local-support requirements
- which equipment and services can be sourced locally
- which costs fall, remain constant or increase as participation grows
- whether model routing or cheaper/open models can reduce costs without materially reducing educational quality

## Principle

Financial sustainability is part of the system architecture. eira should aim for sufficient capability and educational quality while remaining capable of evolving towards a model that can be reproduced and sustained locally if the pilot succeeds.
